# Dry run — M3.12 change management, tested against this repository

Purpose: find out whether the drafted operating test is executable, by running it against a
real population. The workbench's own change record was used, because it is the change record
to hand and because a governance tool that cannot pass its own control test is worth knowing
about.

Population: `governance/tickets.csv`, 15 rows, as at 2026-09-10.

The test is the one in `requirements/mas.yaml` under `test_controls.M3.12`. Five elements:
e1 changes raised including retraining, e2 authorisation before the first production action,
e3 privileged access against the approved change, e3b implementer named on that change,
e4 production state verified against plan.

---

## Result: the test is 40% executable against this record

| Element | Executable? | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| e1 changes raised | yes | pass, with a caveat |
| e2 authorisation before production | **no** | cannot be established at this granularity |
| e3 privileged access to change | **no** | no such data exists |
| e3b named implementer | **no** | no such field exists |
| e4 production state vs plan | **no** | no verification record |

Two of five could be attempted. Three name a population that does not exist. That is a
finding about the test as much as about the record — a test step that cannot be attempted is
not a strict test, it is an unanswerable one, and it will be signed off as "no exceptions
noted" by anyone in a hurry.

## Findings against the record

**F-01. Duplicate rows in the change register.** WB-011, WB-012 and WB-018 each appear twice.
15 rows, 12 distinct tickets. A register with duplicate primary keys cannot support a
population-based test — any sampling or reconciliation double-counts.

**F-02. Authorisation cannot be shown to precede implementation.** `opened_at` and
`approved_at` are dates, not timestamps, and 13 of 15 rows carry the same date in both. The
control asks whether authorisation preceded the first production action. At day granularity,
with no production-action timestamp recorded at all, the question is unanswerable. This is the
single most consequential gap, because e2 is the substance of the control.

**F-03. Six tickets missing from the sequence.** WB-006 to WB-010 and WB-013 are absent.
Either they were never raised, or they were raised and not recorded. The register cannot
distinguish those, which is itself the finding.

**F-04. The register is not current.** Highest ticket is WB-018. The work of 2026-09-09 and
09-10 — the harness rules, the scan-scope fix, the requirement overlay, this reorganisation —
was carried out under references WB-019 to WB-026 and none appear. Changes are being made to
a governed repository ahead of, or without, the register.

**F-05. Inconsistent enumeration.** `emergency` holds both `false` and `FALSE`. Trivial in
itself, but any filter on the emergency population must normalise first, and a test that
forgets to will silently under-sample.

**F-06. No implementer, no privileged-access link, no verification record.** The register
carries `approver` but not who implemented, and nothing links a change to the access used or
to a post-implementation check. e3, e3b and e4 have no data to run against.

## Findings against the test

**T-01. Three of five elements assume artefacts this organisation does not produce.** I wrote
e3, e3b and e4 from your audit experience of a bank with PAM, session recording and
post-implementation verification. This repository has none of that, and neither will most
organisations at the point they first use the workbench. The test needs a stated scope
condition — *where privileged access management is in place* — or it produces "not tested"
against a control that may be adequately managed by other means.

**T-02. e2 needs to specify granularity.** "Authorisation is recorded before the first
production action" is untestable without timestamps and without a recorded production action.
The element should say what evidence would satisfy it: an approval timestamp and a deployment
timestamp, comparable to the minute. As written it invites a reviewer to compare two dates,
see the same day, and pass it.

**T-03. The population statement is wrong for this case.** It says "change register for the
model-serving estate, PAM checkout records and session recordings, one quarter". Two of those
three do not exist here. A population naming sources that may be absent should say what to do
when they are — which is the disposition question still open across the whole set.

**T-04. The dry run took 20 minutes and found more than the second challenge did.** The
challenge document reasoned about the tests; this ran one. Worth remembering when deciding
between another review pass and another dry run.

## What I would change in the test

1. Add a scope condition to e3, e3b and e4: applicable where the artefact exists; otherwise
   record as not applicable with the reason, not as a gap.
2. Rewrite e2 to name the two timestamps it needs and the granularity.
3. Add an element the record actually failed and the test did not ask about: **register
   integrity** — no duplicate ids, no unexplained sequence gaps, and currency against the
   changes actually made. F-01, F-03 and F-04 were all found by looking at the register
   rather than by following the test.

That third point is the useful one. The test was drafted to examine changes; the register
itself turned out to be the weaker control, and nothing in the drafted test would have caught
it.

## What this says about the workbench's own governance

The repository is a governed path with a policy that requires a ticket reference for changes
to `policy/`, and MCM-06 tests it. But the register those tickets live in has duplicates, six
gaps, no entries for two days of substantial change, and no way to establish that approval
preceded implementation.

That is not a reason to stop. It is the same category of finding as the ones from yesterday —
the inert threshold, the disconnected slider, the ignored artefact column — where the
mechanism exists and the evidence behind it does not. Worth a ticket of its own, raised in the
register it is a finding about.
