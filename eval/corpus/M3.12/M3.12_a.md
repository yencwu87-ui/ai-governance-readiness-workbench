# Change Assurance Review — AI/ML production changes, Q2 2026

Technology Risk & Control Assurance
Ref: TRCA-CH-2026-07 | Issued 3 July 2026
Reviewer: L. Fernandez, Technology Risk (2LoD)
Distribution: Head of Data Science Platform, Change Manager, CIO, Internal Audit

---

## Purpose and population

Testing of change controls over production changes affecting deployed models. Population
was all 41 changes raised against the model-serving platform between 1 April and 30 June
2026, taken from the ServiceHub change register rather than from a list supplied by the
platform team. Full population tested, not sampled — the population was small enough.

Population breakdown: 33 normal, 6 emergency, 2 raised during the 20–27 June quarter-end
freeze.

## What was tested and what we found

**Approval before implementation.** All 41 changes carried CAB approval recorded ahead of
the implementation window. For the 6 emergency changes, all 6 carried the on-call duty
manager's approval in the record at the time of implementation, with the retrospective CAB
ratification completed within the two-business-day standard. No change was found where the
approval timestamp post-dated the first production action.

**Change freeze.** Both changes implemented during the 20–27 June freeze carried a freeze
exemption approved by the accountable MD (E. Sandoval, Head of Retail Technology) before
implementation. Both exemptions state the business justification and the consequence of
deferral. Cross-checked the freeze calendar against the change register for the full
quarter; no other changes were implemented inside a declared freeze window.

**Privileged access.** Reconciled the 41 changes against PAM checkout records. Every
production action was performed under a checkout raised against the corresponding change
number, and in every case the checkout requester matched a named implementer on the
approved change. No standing privileged access was used. Two checkouts were opened and not
used — both closed within the window, no action taken.

**Session recording.** All 41 privileged sessions produced a recording retained in the PAM
archive. Spot-checked 8 recordings for completeness against the implementation plan step
list; all 8 played back in full and matched.

**Implemented vs planned.** For 12 changes selected across the three types, compared the
post-implementation verification evidence against the change plan. All 12 matched. In one
case (CHG-0088142) the plan was updated the morning of implementation to add a rollback
step; the amendment carries its own approval and the verification reflects the amended
plan.

**Retraining treated as change.** Confirmed that scheduled retraining runs are raised as
changes rather than treated as business-as-usual. 14 of the 33 normal changes were
retraining deployments.

## Observations

Two, neither rated significant.

1. **OBS-01.** The change record template has no field for the model version being
   deployed; it is captured in free text in the implementation plan. This makes the
   register hard to reconcile to the model inventory without opening each record. Platform
   team to raise a template change. Owner: Change Manager. Target: 30 September 2026.
2. **OBS-02.** Two of the six emergency changes describe the trigger only as "production
   issue". The underlying incidents were located and the emergency classification was
   appropriate in both cases, but the record alone does not evidence it.

## Conclusion

Change controls over AI/ML production changes operated effectively across the quarter for
all attributes tested. No exceptions requiring management action were identified.
